My first blog

Hi, my name is Cameron Kani, and for the start of my new website, I wanted to write my first blog.

I chose to make this website and a blog section on it because my close friend at college inspired me to do so. Honestly, coming from high school and just finishing the college application process, I’m a little burnt out of writing. But I am excited to start it up again. Especially now knowing that I don’t need to write for anyone else. Now, I can just write for myself.

That said, hopefully the writing is clear…I never was a good writer, and definitely will never end up claiming to be.

Anyways, I thought the best first blog should be about my interests here at CMU. The date is September 22, 2026. Just today, CMU was rightfully crowned as the number one university in Computational Biology.

My interest in computer science and AI started a while ago. I remember freshman year of high school. I walked into acting class and noticed a friend had been furiously typing on his computer. Dude, this thing is like Google on steroids is what he said. That thing. The Google on steroids, was—unsurprisingly to whoever is reading this right now—ChatGPT. The coolest new thing. Unlike all other trends, however, this one stuck.

Artificial Intelligence, in any form, has come to define the path of my life, and probably everyone else my age. I’m not sure what was a greater event in my 19 years: COVID or AI. As of right now, I’m torn, but I might end up leaning towards AI as time goes on, since I happen to go to the birthplace of AI (haha MIT. I bet they're salty right now because CMU is now number one in CS).

All that said, ever since I was young, I was always interested in Math and Science. Did you know that pi and e are Universal Numbers? That means these numbers are seen in nature. Some civilization thousands of light years away would notice and calculate (if they haven't already) the exact numbers we call “pi” and “e”. Just, probably with different symbols...and names. (pie? Really?)

My point is, I’m a student at CMU. The number one school for CS, AI, and CompBio. It’s my duty, and the source of my fulfillment, to bring what I learn here to help others. I want to use this powerful new technology to make a difference in someone’s life. I want to share this powerful new technology with everyone around the globe. And I dream of the day I get to say: I used this powerful new technology to save someone’s life.

There’s this story that I learned from a professor here at CMU that has stuck with me ever since. A baby named KJ seemed healthy when he was born. Two days later, he was barely eating, and doctors found a dangerous amount of ammonia in his blood. He had a rare genetic condition that kept his liver from clearing it. For months, his family lived with the possibility that he would need a liver transplant.

Doctors and researchers sequenced his genome (something that once took decades and billions of dollars) and made a treatment for the exact error in KJ’s DNA (a single-letter correction in a genome billions of letters long). They used CRISPR to change one DNA letter, and at six months old, KJ became the first person to receive a personalized gene-editing treatment of this kind. A personalized medical drug specialized and approved for just one child. Incredible. Science fiction like, yet it happened. It happened because of Computational Biologists.

It worked. KJ went home after more than 300 days in the hospital, and by February 2026, he was walking and talking.

I hope you enjoyed reading my first blog. I can't wait to write more.
